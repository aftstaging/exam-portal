​Task 1 – Solution​
​Sub-task (a) – 40%: Digital Data Sources​
​1ai – Identifying digital data sources to investment appraisals​

​A​ ​capital​ ​investment​ ​of​ ​H$15​ ​million​ ​represents​ ​a​ ​significant​ ​allocation​ ​of​ ​Cartn’s​
 ​resources​ ​and​ ​carries​ ​substantial​ ​risk​ ​given​ ​the​ ​innovative​ ​nature​ ​of​ ​digital​ ​coating.​
  ​Standard​ ​investment​ ​appraisals​ ​often​ ​suffer​ ​from​ ​optimism​ ​bias​ ​or​ ​rely​ ​on​ ​static​
​manufacturer estimates.​

​By​ ​leveraging​ ​digital​ ​data​ ​sources,​ ​the​ ​finance​ ​team​ ​can​ ​move​ ​toward​ ​a​ ​more​​dynamic,​
 ​data-driven​​appraisal.​​This​​ensures​​that​​the​​NPV​​calculation​​reflects​​the​​actual​​operational​
​environment of Cartn‘s 27 factories, rather than theoretical ideals.​

​Internal IoT and sensor data from prototype testing​

​Cartn​ ​could​ ​utilise​ ​real-time​ ​data​ ​captured​ ​by​ ​the​ ​Internet​ ​of​ ​Things​ ​(IoT)​ ​sensors​
​currently installed on the Bio-Shield prototype machines.​

​Traditional​ ​estimates​ ​for​ ​operating​ ​costs​ ​are​ ​often​ ​based​ ​on​ ​best-case​ ​manufacturer​
 ​specifications.​ ​By​ ​extracting​ ​data​ ​directly​ ​from​ ​the​ ​prototype’s​ ​sensors,​ ​we​ ​can​ ​obtain​
  ​precise​​measurements​​on​​electricity​​consumption​​per​​unit,​​the​​actual​​speed​​of​​the​​digital​
   ​nozzles,​​and​​the​​exact​​downtime​​caused​​by​​the​​sensor​​calibration​​errors​​noted​​in​​the​​QC​
​report.​

​Instead​ ​of​ ​asking​ ​a​​floor​​manager​​for​​a​​rough​​estimate​​of​​how​​often​​the​​machine​​stops,​
 ​the​​finance​​team​​pulls​​the​​data​​directly​​from​​the​​prototype’s​​cloud​​dashboard.​​This​​digital​
  ​log​​records​​every​​micro-stop​​-​​even​​those​​lasting​​only​​30​​seconds​​-​​that​​a​​human​​operator​
​might fail to record in a manual logbook.​

​For​​example,​​the​​IoT​​data​​could​​reveal​​that​​every​​time​​the​​machine​​switches​​from​​a​​1-litre​
 ​carton​​to​​a​​0.2-litre​​carton,​​the​​digital​​nozzles​​require​​a​​15-minute​​recalibration​​period​​due​
  ​to​ ​sensor​ ​sensitivity.​ ​This​ ​results​ ​in​ ​an​ ​actual​ ​production​ ​speed​ ​of​ ​only​ ​8,500​ ​units​ ​per​
​hour - 15% lower than the manufacturer's best-case claim.​

​This​​allows​​for​​a​​much​​more​​granular​​and​​accurate​​calculation​​of​​operating​​cash​​flows.​​It​
 ​reduces​ ​the​ ​risk​ ​of​ ​the​ ​Board​ ​approving​ ​an​ ​investment​ ​based​ ​on​ ​underestimated​
  ​maintenance​​or​​energy​​costs,​​ensuring​​the​​H$15​​million​​spend​​is​​anchored​​in​​operational​
​reality.​

​Sub-task (b) – 60%: Value management techniques​
​1bi – Recommending quality and value management techniques​

​The​ ​transition​ ​from​ ​traditional​ ​plastic​ ​lamination​ ​to​ ​digital​ ​bio-coating​ ​introduces​ ​both​
 ​financial​ ​and​ ​technical​ ​complexities.​ ​To​ ​ensure​ ​the​ ​Bio-Shield​ ​project​ ​contributes​ ​to​
  ​sustainable​ ​value​ ​creation,​ ​Cartn​ ​must​ ​balance​ ​cost​ ​efficiency​ ​with​ ​uncompromising​
​quality.​

​Value​ ​Engineering​ ​will​​allow​​Cartn​​to​​optimise​​the​​product's​​design​​and​​cost​​base​​before​
 ​full-scale​ ​manufacturing​ ​begins,​ ​while​ ​Total​ ​Quality​ ​Management​ ​(TQM)​ ​will​ ​foster​ ​a​
  ​culture​ ​of​ ​right​ ​first​ ​time,​ ​addressing​ ​the​ ​technical​ ​failures​ ​identified​ ​in​ ​the​ ​recent​ ​QC​
​report.​

​Application of Value Engineering (VE) to coating thickness​

​Cartn​​could​​apply​​value​​engineering​​to​​find​​the​​optimum​​coating​​thickness​​that​​maintains​
​UHT integrity while minimising material use.​

​Value​​engineering​​focuses​​on​​providing​​the​​necessary​​function​​(protecting​​food​​at​​135°C)​
 ​at​​the​​lowest​​cost​​without​​sacrificing​​quality.​​Since​​the​​starch-based​​material​​is​​expensive​
  ​and​ ​thins​ ​during​ ​UHT​ ​processing,​ ​Cartn​ ​engineers​ ​should​ ​collaborate​ ​with​ ​the​ ​finance​
   ​team​ ​to​ ​redesign​ ​the​ ​application​ ​process​ ​-​ ​perhaps​ ​increasing​ ​the​ ​coating​ ​only​ ​at​ ​the​
​critical seams while keeping it thinner on flat surfaces.​

​For​ ​example,​ ​Cartn​ ​should​​perform​​a​​functional​​cost​​analysis​​on​​the​​Bio-Shield​​carton​​to​
​determine if a uniform coating is necessary across the entire surface.​

​Since​​the​​starch-based​​material​​is​​more​​expensive,​​the​​team​​should​​use​​the​​digital​​nozzles​
 ​to​ ​apply​ ​a​ ​thicker,​ ​heat-resistant​ ​layer​ ​only​ ​to​ ​the​ ​seams​ ​and​ ​seals​ ​that​ ​failed​ ​at​ ​135°C,​
​while maintaining a thinner layer on the flat panels.​

​This​ ​prevents​ ​over-engineering​ ​and​ ​keeps​ ​the​ ​unit​ ​cost​ ​of​ ​the​ ​Bio-Shield​ ​carton​
 ​competitive.​ ​By​ ​optimising​ ​the​ ​material​ ​distribution,​ ​Cartn​ ​can​ ​resolve​ ​the​ ​technical​
  ​failures​​identified​​in​​the​​QC​​report​​without​​further​​bloating​​the​​production​​cost,​​ensuring​
   ​the​​H$15​​million​​potential​​investment​​delivers​​a​​better​​outcome​​compared​​to​​a​​standard,​
​non-optimised production line.​

​TQM: Quality at source and empowerment​

​Cartn​ ​could​ ​also​ ​implement​ ​quality​ ​at​ ​source​ ​by​ ​training​ ​factory​ ​operators​ ​to​ ​interpret​
​the digital nozzle sensor data in real-time.​

​In​​this​​respect,​​total​​quality​​management​​(TQM)​​moves​​away​​from​​inspecting-in​​quality​​at​
 ​the​​end​​of​​the​​line​​and​​builds​​it​​into​​the​​product.​​Operators​​should​​be​​empowered​​to​​halt​
  ​production​​and​​recalibrate​​sensors​​the​​moment​​the​​digital​​dashboard​​shows​​a​​deviation.​
​This shifts the culture from doing what we're told to one of professional ownership.​

​For​​example,​​Cartn​​could​​implement​​Statistical​​Process​​Control​​(SPC)​​dashboards​​at​​every​
 ​Bio-Shield​ ​workstation,​ ​giving​ ​operators​ ​the​ ​autonomy​​to​​halt​​production​​the​​moment​​a​
​sensor drift is detected.​

​By​ ​shifting​ ​the​ ​responsibility​ ​for​ ​quality​ ​from​ ​a​ ​separate​ ​inspection​ ​department​ ​to​ ​the​
 ​operators​ ​themselves,​ ​Cartn​ ​addresses​ ​and​ ​potentially​ ​lowers​ ​the​ ​4%​ ​leakage​ ​rate​
​mentioned in the QC report before it becomes a mass-production failure.​

​This​​reduces​​internal​​failure​​costs​​of​​scrap​​and​​rework,​​while​​supporting​​Cartn’s​​long-term​
 ​sustainability​ ​strategy.​ ​By​ ​ensuring​ ​that​​quality​​is​​built-in​​rather​​than​​inspected-in,​​Cartn​
  ​minimises​ ​the​ ​carbon​ ​footprint​ ​of​ ​wasted​ ​starch-materials​ ​and​ ​energy,​ ​helping​ ​the​
​company reach its goal of reducing emissions below the current 24.0 g CO2e per carton​

​Total Quality Management: Kaizen and continuous improvement​

​Another​ ​element​ ​of​ ​total​ ​quality​ ​management​ ​would​ ​be​ ​to​ ​establish​ ​a​ ​Kaizen​ ​Feedback​
 ​Loop​​between​​the​​800-strong​​consultancy​​division​​and​​the​​manufacturing​​factories.​​This​
​draws on the total quality management philosophy of continuous improvement.​

​Cartn‘s​​consultants,​​who​​design​​packaging​​equipment​​at​​client​​sites,​​will​​see​​firsthand​​how​
 ​the​ ​Bio-Shield​ ​cartons​ ​perform​ ​in​ ​real-world​ ​conditions.​ ​This​ ​data​​should​​be​​fed​​back​​to​
  ​the​​director​​of​​product​​development​​to​​make​​incremental​​software​​updates​​to​​the​​digital​
​nozzles.​

​For​ ​example,​ ​Cartn​ ​could​ ​establish​ ​a​ ​formal​ ​monthly​ ​Field-to-Factory​ ​forum​ ​where​
 ​consultancy​ ​engineers​ ​share​ ​data​ ​on​ ​how​ ​Bio-Shield​ ​cartons​ ​perform​​during​​high-speed​
​filling at client sites.​

​By​​Cartn​​using​​their​​800​​consultancy​​staff​​as​​eyes​​and​​ears​​in​​the​​field,​​they​​can​​identify​
 ​real-world​ ​issues​ ​-​ ​such​ ​as​ ​the​ ​thinning​ ​at​ ​135°C​ ​occurring​ ​more​ ​frequently​ ​on​ ​certain​
  ​types​​of​​filling​​machinery​​-​​and​​feed​​this​​back​​to​​the​​manufacturing​​team​​for​​immediate,​
​incremental adjustments to the digital nozzle settings.​

​Task 2 – Solution​
​Sub-task (a) – 60%: Business Models​
​2ai – Evaluate circular business model​

​The​ ​transition​ ​to​ ​a​ ​circular​ ​business​ ​model​ ​represents​ ​a​ ​shift​ ​from​ ​selling​ ​a​ ​physical​
 ​product​ ​to​ ​managing​ ​a​​resource's​​lifecycle.​​For​​Cartn,​​this​​means​​moving​​away​​from​​the​
  ​linear​​consumption​​of​​paperboard​​and​​aluminium​​and​​instead​​creating​​value​​by​​retaining​
​ownership or oversight of the materials after the consumer has used them.​

​Resource efficiency and cost decoupling​

​The​ ​circular​ ​model​ ​creates​ ​value​​by​​decoupling​​Cartn's​​growth​​from​​the​​volatility​​of​​raw​
 ​material​ ​prices.​ ​As​ ​in​ ​a​ ​linear​ ​model,​ ​Cartn​​has​​to​​take​​the​​market​​price​​for​​paperboard​
  ​and​ ​aluminium.​ ​However,​ ​by​ ​recovering​ ​fibres​ ​through​ ​the​ ​'Take-Back'​ ​scheme,​ ​Cartn​
​creates a secondary internal supply chain.​

​For​ ​example,​ ​Cartn​ ​can​ ​use​ ​the​ ​Take-Back​ ​scheme​ ​to​ ​create​ ​a​ ​natural​ ​hedge​ ​against​
 ​volatility​ ​in​ ​the​ ​global​ ​paper​ ​pulp​ ​market.​ ​By​ ​recovering​ ​fibres​ ​from​ ​used​ ​Bio-Shield​
  ​cartons,​​Cartn​​builds​​a​​secondary​​inventory​​of​​raw​​materials.​​This​​allows​​the​​company​​to​
   ​substitute​​expensive​​raw​​materials​​with​​recovered​​fibres​​in​​non-food-contact​​applications,​
​such as the outer structural layers of frozen food tubs or transit packaging.​

​This​ ​decouples​ ​Cartn’s​​profit​​margins​​from​​external​​commodity​​price​​shocks.​​If​​pulp​​and​
 ​other​​raw​​material​​prices​​rise​​by​​20%,​​Cartn’s​​cost​​of​​sales​​only​​rises​​by​​a​​fraction​​of​​that​
  ​amount​​because​​they​​are​​sourcing​​a​​portion​​of​​their​​raw​​material​​from​​their​​own​​recycled​
   ​loop.​ ​This​ ​protects​ ​profit​ ​margins​ ​and​ ​ensures​ ​Cartn​ ​remains​ ​the​ ​lowest-cost​ ​provider​
​even during resource shortages.​

​Brand differentiation and customer loyalty​

​This​ ​model​ ​enhances​ ​the​ ​perceived​ ​quality​ ​of​ ​Cartn’s​ ​products,​ ​allowing​ ​for​ ​a​ ​price​
 ​premium​ ​and​ ​deeper​ ​customer​ ​integration.​ ​By​ ​partnering​ ​with​ ​retailers​ ​for​ ​collection,​
  ​Cartn​ ​becomes​ ​an​ ​essential​ ​part​ ​of​ ​the​ ​retailer's​ ​own​ ​Environmental,​ ​Social,​ ​and​
   ​Governance​ ​(ESG)​ ​targets.​ ​This​ ​stickiness​ ​makes​ ​it​ ​harder​ ​for​ ​competitors​ ​to​ ​displace​
​Cartn.​

​For​​example,​​Cartn​​can​​use​​the​​Bio-Shield​​range​​to​​transition​​from​​a​​transactional​​supplier​
 ​to​ ​a​ ​strategic​ ​ESG​ ​partner​ ​for​ ​major​ ​retailers​ ​like​ ​Harr-Foods.​ ​By​ ​providing​ ​a​ ​Take-Back​
  ​infrastructure​ ​and​ ​the​ ​associated​ ​environmental​ ​impact​ ​data,​ ​Cartn​ ​aligns​ ​its​ ​business​
​model with the retailer's corporate sustainability goals.​

​​Mitigation of end-of-life sustainability risks​

​The​​circular​​business​​model​​addresses​​the​​recycling​​difficulty​​in​​Harrland​​and​​reduces​​the​
​risk of future plastic taxes or legislative penalties.​

​Cartn's​​traditional​​cartons​​are​​a​​laminated​​sandwich​​of​​paper,​​aluminium,​​and​​plastic,​​and​
 ​these​ ​are​ ​often​ ​dumped​ ​in​ ​a​ ​landfill​​site​​because​​local​​governments​​lack​​the​​facilities​​to​
​separate them.​

​By​ ​taking​ ​responsibility​ ​for​ ​the​ ​end-of-life​ ​disposal,​​Cartn​​preemptively​​manages​​the​​risk​
​that its products will be banned or heavily taxed by the Harrlandian government.​

​Legislators​ ​are​ ​increasingly​ ​shifting​ ​the​ ​financial​ ​burden​ ​of​ ​waste​ ​disposal​ ​from​ ​the​
 ​taxpayer​​to​​the​​manufacturer.​​By​​taking​​responsibility​​for​​the​​end-of-life​​of​​the​​Bio-Shield​
  ​cartons​ ​now,​ ​Cartn​ ​ensures​ ​its​ ​business​ ​model​ ​remains​ ​viable​ ​even​ ​if​ ​the​ ​government​
​introduces heavy landfill levies or plastic taxes on non-recycled packaging.​

​This​​creates​​financial​​value​​for​​shareholders​​by​​shielding​​the​​company​​from​​unpredictable​
 ​legal​ ​liabilities​ ​and​ ​tax​ ​penalties.​ ​It​ ​also​ ​addresses​ ​the​ ​recycling​ ​difficulty​ ​in​ ​Harrland,​
  ​effectively​​future-proofing​​Cartn's​​market​​share​​against​​competitors​​who​​remain​​stuck​​in​
​an unsustainable linear model that may eventually be banned or taxed out of existence.​

​Cartn​ ​must​ ​recognise​ ​a​ ​provision​ ​for​ ​the​ ​H$3.2​ ​million​ ​clean-up​ ​cost​ ​under​ ​IAS​ ​37​
​Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets.​

​obligation.​

​With​​that​​in​​mind,​​Cartn​​must​​record​​the​​obligation​​at​​its​​present​​value.​​Since​​the​​H$3.2​
 ​million​​is​​payable​​in​​two​​years,​​we​​would​​discount​​this​​figure​​using​​a​​pre-tax​​discount​​rate.​
  ​If​ ​we​ ​assume​ ​a​ ​discount​ ​rate​ ​of​ ​5%,​ ​Cartn​ ​would​ ​record​ ​an​ ​initial​ ​provision​ ​of​
​approximately H$2.9 million today.​

​In​ ​terms​ ​of​ ​impact​ ​on​ ​the​ ​financial​ ​statements,​ ​Cartn​ ​will​ ​recognise​ ​a​ ​H$2.9​ ​million​
 ​liability​​immediately​​that​​will​​increase​​Cartn's​​debt-to-equity​​ratio.​​If​​Cartn​​has​​strict​​bank​
​covenants regarding debt levels, this could technically put those covenants at risk.​

​On​​the​​profit​​and​​loss​​statement,​​Cartn​​will​​recognise​​an​​immediate​​H$2.9​​million​​expense​
 ​for​ ​the​ ​initial​ ​provision.​ ​Additionally,​ ​as​ ​the​ ​payment​ ​date​ ​moves​ ​closer,​ ​Cartn​ ​will​
  ​recognise​ ​a​​finance​​cost​​(unwinding​​of​​the​​discount),​​which​​will​​marginally​​impact​​profit​
​over the next two years.​

​By​ ​recognising​ ​this​ ​now,​ ​Cartn​ ​are​ ​essentially​ ​pre-paying​ ​the​ ​reputational​ ​cost​ ​of​ ​the​
